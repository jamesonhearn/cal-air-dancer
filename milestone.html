Air Dancer

Single-chamber air and cloth pressure integration

Video Slides Paper (PDF) Webpage Proposal

Abstract and Team

An air dancer is a fabric tube on a blower whose flailing comes from a feedback loop: airflow deforms the tube wall, and the deformed wall changes the airflow [1]. We aim to reproduce that motion by simulating the loop, coupling a reduced airflow model to the C-IPC cloth and contact solver [3] rather than animating the result. At this milestone we have merged the first half of that coupling: a single-chamber air model, tube volume computed from the cloth mesh, and pressure forces applied inside C-IPC. Standalone tests confirm conservation, equilibria, and timestep convergence, and short coupled runs complete inside C-IPC.

1. Design and Validation

Air dancers combine large cloth deformation, self-contact, internal pressure, and moving airflow. Our working hypothesis, from the proposal [5], is that a kink narrows the airflow, pressure builds below it while the section above deflates, and the rising pressure straightens the kink so the cycle repeats. Experiments on soft air-conveying tubes have characterized related instabilities [1, 2].

Rather than a full three-dimensional fluid solve, we are building a reduced air model in stages. The first stage, presented here, treats the tube interior as one chamber at uniform pressure. This captures inflation and the fan/outlet balance, and it is also the uniform-pressure control planned in the proposal. The next stage splits the tube into a chain of air sections whose connecting flow depends on the local passage cross-section, so that a kink restricts flow. It reuses the geometry and coupling path built for this milestone.

Contribution and validation

Our contribution is a reduced airflow model for a deforming fabric tube, evaluated by direct comparison with physical references rather than by visual plausibility alone. We will build two configurations.

2. Implemented Method

Cloth backend. A dancing tube folds, kinks, and presses against itself, and the air model depends on that geometry staying valid: a self-intersecting mesh has no meaningful enclosed volume. The HW4 mass-spring simulator was not designed for robust self-collision in tight folds, and building that capability ourselves did not fit the project timeline. We therefore use C-IPC [3], which extends incremental potential contact to codimensional shells and rods. It guarantees intersection-free trajectories, enforces a thickness offset so shell mid-surfaces never come closer than a set distance, and provides strain limiting and frictional contact.

Air chamber. The state is the gas mass m. Absolute pressure follows the isothermal ideal-gas law p = mRT/V, and the fabric is loaded by gauge pressure pg = p − patm. The fan delivers less flow as back-pressure rises and shuts off at pfan,max. The outlet is modeled as an orifice whose flow grows with √pg and reverses when the chamber drops below atmospheric. Mass is advanced with backward-Euler substeps. Fan and outlet parameters are currently synthetic: 800 Pa shutoff, 1.5 m³/s free flow, and a 0.0707 m² outlet.

Geometry. The tube is open at both ends, so to measure its volume we close each boundary ring with a temporary fan of triangles and sum signed tetrahedra. These closing caps exist only for the volume calculation and receive no force. Each fabric triangle receives pgAn, split equally among its three vertices.

C-IPC adapter. Each step, a Python adapter samples the cloth positions, computes volume and outlet area, advances the chamber, and writes gravity plus pressure traction into C-IPC's nodal force vector. The loads are held fixed through C-IPC's implicit solve, and the resulting geometry feeds the next air update (Figure 2).

Air model. Steady pressures agree with an independent analytic solution to within 10−6 Pa, and the restricted case sits just below the 800 Pa fan shutoff, as expected. Each timestep halving roughly halves the error against a fine reference run, as expected for first-order integration. Sealed compression follows the isothermal pV invariant, a fully blocked outlet drives pressure to shutoff and recovers when reopened, and a sub-atmospheric chamber refills through the outlet.

Geometry and coupling. On a meshed cylinder (r = 0.15 m, h = 5 m, 20 × 32 vertices) the computed volume matches the exact prism volume and is unchanged by rigid motion. Summed vertex force magnitude relative to pg times lateral area is cos(π/32) = 0.995, the expected faceting factor, and vertex forces match a finite-difference volume gradient. With stand-in mechanics, the adapter replaces rather than accumulates loads, leaves the floor unloaded, and isolates failed steps. In C-IPC itself, prescribed-pressure and chamber runs each completed eight native substeps (0.067 s) with a chamber mass residual of 4.2 × 10−14 kg. For the full-scale comparison, open and restricted 10 ft profiles (3.048 m × 0.3048 m, the restricted one with a quarter-area fabric outlet) generate 1,200-vertex, 2,360-triangle tube meshes.

4. Progress and Updated Plan

The proposal planned a chain of air sections on the HW4 cloth simulator and targeted a stable inflated tube and a first collapse by this milestone [5]. Two things changed. The cloth side moved to C-IPC for the reasons in Section 2, and validation is now framed around the two physical references in Section 1. The air model merged so far is the single chamber, which doubles as the planned uniform-pressure control. The coupling path is complete and tested, but coupled runs cover only the first frames, so sustained inflation, shape-dependent flow, and collapse are behind the Week 2 schedule.
